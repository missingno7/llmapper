**P (first sheet)** reads as an underground railway concourse, with a ticket window, broad stairs and a long iron-grilled enclosure.  
**Q (second sheet)** reads as a haunted railway terminus, with a platform, a green passenger train and a waiting room.

**Q is the stronger place overall, 4/5 to P’s 3/5.**

| Criterion | P | Q |
|---|---:|---:|
| Composition | 3 | 4 |
| Light | 2 | 3 |
| Scale | 4 | 4 |
| Detail | 3 | 5 |
| Readability | 3 | 4 |

P’s wide stairs and flanking aisles sell the scale of a public concourse, but flat lighting lets the repeated grilles dominate. Q uses brighter carriage openings to frame the passengers, and the wheels, benches and signs make the station legible at a glance. Its dark track approach is the weakest view.

- **P has** a staffed-looking ticket window, broad stair flights, a skull on a pedestal and a continuous iron barrier that splits the hall.
- **Q has** visible train wheels and a carriage threshold, seated dead passengers, a waiting room with luggage and a clock, and an end-on view of car 666.
- **Thin copy?** Neither reads as one. The closest match is P’s row of rusty piers and green-backed bays against Q’s platform-side carriage openings. The resemblance is strongest in the repeated bays.
- **Blocking faults:** None visible in either set. Q’s end-on shot makes the route hard to read, but the stills cannot establish whether passage is blocked.
