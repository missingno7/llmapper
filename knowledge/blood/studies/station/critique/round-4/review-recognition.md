# Round 4, recognition reader (station, mute frames)

## 1. What is it
"A small railway station: platform beside a parked train." Sure: 4. Frames 5 (carpeted, curtained corridor full of crates) and 9 (an open street with a clock on a facade) read as different places: a hotel or theatre corridor, and a town square.

## 2. What made me say that
- Rails with sleepers in frames 2, 3 and 7. The green-and-silver box parked beside the platform with wheel trucks under it (frame 2) reads as a passenger car.
- A pillared hall with wooden benches, a seated figure and the car visible through the arches (frame 4) reads as a waiting room.
- A barred window with a man behind it next to a door (frame 8) reads as a ticket office.
- Confusing: the "locomotive" in frames 6 and 7 is a diamond-plate box with wheel arches. It reads as a crate or a low wall, not an engine. In frame 1 the car is a flat green slab with brown door-like panels for windows, so it reads as a hedge or a fence. In frame 3 the tracks lie in a slot open to a red sky between sheer brick walls, so they read as a canal or an alley. Frame 5 has no link to trains unless it is the inside of a carriage, and it is far too long and full of crates for that.

## 3. Expected but missing
- A platform edge line, lamps and a canopy or train-shed roof over the tracks.
- A timetable board, luggage and trolleys, and buffer stops.
- A gap or coupling between cars, and doors on the cars.
- A chimney, boiler and cab on the engine.

## 4. Needs imagination
- That the grey riveted block is a locomotive.
- That frame 5 is a carriage interior.
- That frame 9's facade is the station front. Nothing marks it as one: it is any city building.

## 5. Character: 2
The waiting hall with benches is the only memorable view. Everything else is long brick corridors.

## 6. Blocking
- The locomotive is an unreadable riveted box: it has no boiler, chimney or cab.
- Frame 5's crate-filled carpeted corridor does not read as part of a station or a train.
