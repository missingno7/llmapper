# Station, round 4: the use reader

## 1. As a passenger
- **Finding it (view_09).** STATION is lettered in green across a neighbour's windowed facade. It reads as a shop sign. The door itself is a bare dark slot 3.4 m wide and 0.25 m deep, with no frame, canopy or lintel name. The lamps and clock help. You can find it, but the doorway does not say "station".
- **Getting in.** The PASSAGE is 5.1 x 6.8 m with a 6.6 m dark ceiling, and the RAMP climbs 2.05 m over 6.8 m. That is a service tunnel, not a station forecourt.
- **Ticket (view_08).** This is the strongest moment. A TICKETS board hangs over a barred window 1.7 m wide with a sill at 0.8 m, a clerk stands behind it, and a side door is open. It reads at once.
- **Waiting (view_04).** The HALL is 17 x 10.2 x 5 m, with two benches, lamp pools, gates and a carriage visible beyond. It works. The benches still read as crates: the back is only 0.2 m behind the seat and wears the same tile.
- **Platform and train.** The platform is 3.4 m wide under the canopy. Seen from it (view_01), it is a brick corridor beside a green wall. The livery is still the noisy felt-green, and the windows read as brown doors. There is no edge line, no nameboard facing the track (GRAVESEND hangs on the hall's ramp wall), and no platform number.
- **Boarding.** The door is shut and nothing says why. Inside (view_05), the curtains and carpet sell it.
- **Where it goes.** TO THE QUAY is a small, clipped notice (view_07). The locomotive's nose stops 3.7 m short of a barred tunnel. The train can never leave.

## 2. Four things, in order
1. **The line goes somewhere.** Open the tunnel to the quay as the exit, so the station is a passage and not a cul-de-sac.
2. **A locomotive that reads as one.** It needs a round boiler, a smokebox door, a tender with coal and a lamp on the buffer beam. Today it is stacked diamond-plate boxes (view_06/07).
3. **A signal box or stationmaster's window over the platform.** Put the lever there, so the lever and the door can be seen together.
4. **A harbour's freight.** Fish crates, a luggage trolley, trunks and a timetable naming Gravesend Quay.

## 3. Sizes
- The carriage door is 1.7 m wide; a real one is about 0.7 m. The car itself (18.4 x 3.4 m outside, 2.55 m inside, 2.5 m clear) is right.
- The boiler is a 2.6 m square box standing 3.1 m tall. A real boiler is about 1.5 m round.
- The platform stands 1.44 m above the rail; a real one is about 0.9 m.
- The far-track sky is a slot between walls of near street height (view_03), so the "open air" feels like a well.
- The passage is 6.6 m high for a 5.1 m corridor.

## 4. As a player
The sequence is: clerk behind bars, then round the side door, kill the clerk, pull the lever (OFFICE west wall), walk about 14 m to the carriage, where a guard and a porter defend the prize. It has a beginning, the window with the clerk, and an end, the prize. Three things weaken it:
- The lever's effect happens about 14 m away, out of sight. Nothing ties the lever to the carriage.
- Every other way leads to a dead end: the ramp down to the track, the buffer, and the barred tunnel.
- Once the prize is taken, you walk all the way back out to the street.

Two cheap improvements:
- **(a)** Make the carriage door keyed, and have the clerk drop the key. The ticket becomes the key. Alternatively, move the lever to the platform within sight of the door.
- **(b)** When the door opens, trigger a second wave: the loiterer and cultists come up the ramp from the track. The way back becomes a fight.

## 5. This town?
Mostly generic. The sacks and barrels, the TO THE QUAY notice and the Pandemonium Show bill nod to Gravesend. Nothing smells of a harbour: no fish, nets or gulls, and no quay at the tunnel's end.

## 6. Score: 3
The booking window and the carriage interior are memorable. The train outside and the dead-end line are not.

## 7. Blocking
- The office lever opens a carriage door about 14 m away and out of sight, with no cue linking them. Pulling it appears to do nothing.
