# Station, round 6: use (passenger, then player)

## 1. Passenger
- **Finding it (view 11):** green STATION letters sit high on the neighbour's fascia, east of the door. The doorway itself (DOOR, 3.4 m wide and 3.3 m high) is a black void with a thin frame. It has no canopy, no lamp of its own and no name over it, because the passage behind it is shade 38-50. The clock and the lamp sprite (reads as a framed eye) hang on the wrong building. I would walk past it.
- **Ticket (view 10):** this works. TICKETS sign, a barred window 1.7 m wide with a 0.8 m sill, a clerk behind it, and a side door. There is no ledge or counter on my side, but it reads.
- **Waiting (views 5-6):** the hall (17 x 10 m, 5 m high) has the right size and mood. The benches face the trains but read as solid brown crates from the hall. The pools of light show as orange squares on the ceiling, not on the floor. There is no waiting room and nowhere warm.
- **Platform and train (views 2-3):** reached through two open gate bays, which is clear. The platform is 3.4 m wide under the hall's own roof, so it reads as a corridor. There is no running-in board facing the train, no platform lamp and no departure board. GRAVESEND hangs on the hall's south wall, behind me.
- **Boarding (view 1):** the red door with its window and the JO-JO bill is convincing, but it is 1.7 m wide (a coach door is about 0.7 m). The car roof meets the shed planks with no gap (CAR_TOP = SHED_CLEAR), so from the platform it is a striped wall.
- **Where it goes:** "TO THE QUAY" is lettered edge-on beside the locomotive and half hidden by it (view 9). The tunnel is a barred black hole under a strip of red sky that reads as a lid (views 4 and 9).

## 2. Four things that would do most here
1. A station front on the street: a porch or canopy over the door, the name board directly above it, and a lit lamp in the passage.
2. A running-in board saying GRAVESEND, facing the train, with two hanging lamps and a clock under the canopy.
3. Harbour freight on the platform: a luggage or fish trolley, fish boxes, and a parcels van coupled behind the coach so there is a *train*, not one car.
4. A signal and a water crane at the tunnel mouth, so the line visibly runs on toward the quay.

## 3. Sizes

- Carriage: 3.4 m wide (real 2.7-2.9 m) and 17.9 m long, with no clearance under the shed roof.
- Platform: 1.4 m above the rail (real about 0.9 m); 3.4 m wide is the minimum.
- Locomotive chimney: stands 1.85 m above a square boiler, so its top is 4.9 m over the rail (real about 4 m). It reads as a pillar.
- Locomotive cab: only 1.8 m from the coach, with nothing coupling them.
- Hall, office (3.0 x 5.1 m), benches (0.46 m seat, 0.64 m deep) and tunnel (12 x 6.6 m) are all fine.

## 4. Player
The sequence: kill the loiterer in the hall and the clerk (side door), take the office loot, pull the lever, then fight the guard and porter in the carriage for the prize at its east end.
- It has a beginning but no end. The lever stands on the column right beside the door, so the puzzle is one press with no cost.
- The car, described as "a way through", is sealed at both ends.
- The ramp down to the track leads to an empty walk ending at bars. After the prize, you backtrack.
- Nothing I saw would break it. The 0.2 m strip of shed floor between car and platform is too narrow to fall into. No frame shows the lever, so I cannot confirm it is visible.

Cheap improvements:
1. Lock the carriage door with a key the clerk holds, so the office comes first and the door is a reward.
2. Put a switch in the carriage's far end, or in the locomotive cab, that opens the tunnel grille. Even a short bore with loot gives the visit a way out instead of a backtrack.

## 5. This town or generic?
Mostly generic E1M2/E1M3 vocabulary. Nothing says harbour (no fish, nets, crates for ships or quay smell) except one sign behind the engine. The JO-JO bill ties it to the Pandemonium Show, which is good.
