## Recognition read, station round 6

1. **A small railway station with a green passenger car and a steam locomotive at the platform.** Sureness 4. Frame 1 alone reads as a green shopfront or building facade with a door. Frame 11 is a city street outside, not the station.

2. What told me this:
- Rails on cobble in frames 3, 4 and 9. These are the strongest signal.
- The long green carriage seen from track level in frame 4, with its undercarriage wheels in frame 3.
- The dark locomotive with drive wheels and a stack in frames 8 and 9.
- The pillared hall with barrels in frames 2 and 5 reads as a platform.
- The tunnel mouth at the end of the tracks.
- Frame 7 is a parlour-car interior with curtains, chairs and a table. It is convincing.

What confused me:
- The carriage windows are opaque wooden panels. From the platform (frames 1, 2 and 5) the car looks like a painted wall with picture frames, not a vehicle you could see into.
- The car's door in frame 1 is a house door.
- The waiting-room "benches" in frames 5 and 6 are featureless wooden blocks that read as crates or a counter.
- Frame 10, a barred window beside an open doorway, could be a ticket office or a jail.

3. What I expected but did not find:
- A ticket window that is clearly one.
- A platform edge or kerb with a drop to the track.
- Lamps or light pools on the platform. Everything is evenly dim.
- A canopy edge that faces the sky.
- Luggage or carts, and a buffer stop.
- Steam or soot on the locomotive.
- Any sense of where the line goes; the tunnel is a black slab.

4. What needs imagination:
- That the wooden boxes are benches.
- That frame 10 is a ticket office.
- That the green wall seen from the platform is the side of a train. Without frames 3 and 4 I would not accept it.

5. Character score: 3. The locomotive with its gargoyle face and the parlour car are memorable. The platform hall is generic brick and pillars.

6. Blocking defects:
- The carriage windows are solid wood panels, so from the platform the train reads as a building wall and not a vehicle.
- The tunnel mouth is a flat black rectangle with no depth, so the line goes nowhere.
