# Recognition read, station round 2

**1. Place:** a small underground railway station with a parked train car. Sureness 4. Not every frame matches: view 6 reads as a shop or pantry aisle with a vendor behind a crate, and view 9 is an outdoor city street with no link to a station.

**2. What told me:**
- The rails with wooden sleepers on cobble (views 2, 3) and the black tunnel mouth (view 3) say railway.
- The green box with a window band, a corrugated skirt and bogie wheels (views 2, 3, 7) is plainly a carriage.
- The colonnade of riveted iron pillars under a brick barrel vault (views 4, 5) and the barred window with a clerk behind it (view 8) read as a concourse and a ticket office.
- What confused me: the wooden blocks in view 4/5 could be benches, crates or counters, so I read them as baggage crates. The robed figures behind them read as customers or monks. The shelved aisle in view 6 looks like a general store, not a station kiosk. The carriage window in view 1 shows bare brick, so it reads as a hole in a box.

**3. Expected but missing:**
- A raised platform edge dropping down to the track bed. Everything here is one flush floor, so the carriage sits on cobble beside the rails, not on them.
- Buffers or a track end, platform lamps and a clock.
- Benches you can recognise as benches, luggage, and a canopy or roof trusses.
- Anything through the carriage windows: seats or an interior.
- Signals or a second track.

**4. Only accepted if told:**
- View 9 is part of a station; it is just a street.
- View 6 is a station kiosk.
- The wooden blocks are benches or counters.
- View 1's corridor is a platform; it is about one body wide.
- The carriage is a carriage, not a green crate, when seen end-on (view 7).

**5. Character: 2/5.** The vault and pillars are decent, but it is all one dim brown-and-grey palette with nothing memorable.

**6. Blocking:**
- No platform edge: the floor is flush with the track bed, so there is no platform.
- The carriage is not on the rails and reads as a box parked on cobble.
- Carriage windows are see-through to bare brick, with no interior.
- View 9 (the street) and view 6 (the shop aisle) do not read as part of a station.
- A poster with legible lettering is still visible in view 6, although lettering was meant to be removed.
