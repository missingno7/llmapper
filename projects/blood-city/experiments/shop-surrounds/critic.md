# Blind review: five unlabelled versions of the shopfronts

One fresh reviewer (Opus), five folders of the same nine views and nothing
else: P = unnamed, Q = before, R = full, S = unfronted, T = unlit. It was not
told which was which, only that some "may differ from each other in only one
respect". Interpretation, not measurement. Condensed; quotations are its
words.

## What it worked out

"Three things vary, each switched on or off independently": the shop
surround, the sign, the glass treatment. It tabulated all five sets
correctly and named the pairs that differ in one respect: "P and R: the
sign. R and S: the surround. R and T: the glass."

- "Sign: decisive. It turns the pawn from a dress shop into a pawnbroker and
  the super from a storeroom into a grocer. It is weak at distance."
- "Surround: helps. Without it the window reads as a hole in a residential
  or tech facade, and the sign has nothing to sit on."
- "Dark glass: hurts, mildly. It is slightly more believable as glass, but
  it hides the goods, which are what identifies a shop."

(It read the unlit rooms as tinted glass. The glass is the same in every
set; what differs is the light behind it.)

## Still wrong in the best set

1. "The mall units sell nothing ... empty tiled cubicles with a lantern on a
   long chain dropping almost to the floor."
2. "The super reads as a back room once you are past the sign": the lantern
   "mid-doorway at eye level in front of a crate"; "PROVISIONS" "dim blue on
   black and illegible" from far; `super_south_pane` "shows a flat orange
   brick panel, not an interior".
3. "The pawn's goods say 'boutique', not 'pawn'. Every item is clothing on
   mannequins."

## Faults it listed

Nearly all in S (unfronted), which is the point of that variant: "the sign
band slices the upper-floor windows in half"; "brick-window texture is
squashed onto the narrow pier faces"; "a stretched dark sci-fi panel
texture" on the centre mullion. In Q and T: "the streaks look like scratches
on the screen". In all sets: "PAWNBROKER" is cropped by the frame's edge in
`pawn_front`.
