# Blind review, round 3 (3.9-body gables, ridge 17.4; portal 2.4 bodies)

Reviewer: a fresh model instance, no memory of rounds 1 or 2, given only the
twelve frames in `after/frames/` under the same instructions. One reviewer,
one pass: an interpretation, not a measurement.

## What it said

- vista_far: "The best frame. A slender dark spire with a lit window...
  **Church steeple (certain as a silhouette)**." But: "the spire seems to sit
  behind or apart from the grey church, and it's unclear if it is the same
  building."
- avenue_south: "Church (likely), from the stained glass plus tower."
- portal: "a reasonable heavy wooden door in a deeper recess with a lintel.
  **Church door (likely)**." Still: "barely bigger than a house door".
- avenue_south_up: "a plain square shaft... no spire, no roof and no belfry
  openings; it just ends flat against the sky. Bell tower (guess)."
- tower_foot: "reads as a fortress or prison wall rather than a church".
- cemetery_side: "Cemetery wall / chapel side (guess)."

## Its three largest faults

1. Nothing has a roof: every building ends at a flat parapet, the church has
   no gable, "the steeple seen from afar does not match the tower seen up
   close".
2. Huge blank repetitive planes; scale reads wrong.
3. Black voids at the street ends, and sky-texture spires competing with the
   real landmark.

## What this refutes

The author looked at round 3 and wrote "reads as a steeple". From the market
end and the spawn, yes. From the foot of it, no: the gable is 3.9 bodies of
slope at the top of a 13.5-body shaft seen from 5 plan units away, and from
there the top edge is foreshortened flat. The far read and the near read are
two different problems and round 3 solved one.

The near read needs things at eye height and just above: a plinth, a door or
louvres in the tower's own foot, a change of tile up the shaft. None was
tried.
