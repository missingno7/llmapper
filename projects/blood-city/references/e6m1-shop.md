# E6M1 shop — extracted prefab language

Source data: [e6m1-shop.json](e6m1-shop.json), generated by
`python -m tools.mine_e6m1_shop`.  Rendered source views are generated with
`tools.render_precedent` for sectors 45, 50, 61, 63 and 79.  Role names below
come from the owner's inspection; coordinates, z values, sprite fields and
counts are measurements from `maps/blood/E6M1.MAP`.

## It is a department-store composition, not a prop-scattered room

The public shop is made from distinct, readable zones:

- **S45, apparel bay** — 6,144 × 9,472.  Nine wall-aligned clothes sprites
  (tile 73, `cstat 144`, 64×64) sit at a common height of 14,336 above the
  floor, using palettes 11, 12, 13 and 14.  Shelf-wall texture 2635 occurs
  twice in the same bay.  This is the dense rack aisle in the source render.
- **S50, display window** — 6,656 × 1,792.  Three mannequins (tile 2377,
  56×56, palettes 12/13/11) stand independently of the customer floor at
  10,976 above its floor.  Window display is its own volume, not a mannequin
  planted in the shop aisle.
- **S61/S63, selling floor** — S61 is 4,608 × 12,544 and S63 is
  4,096 × 7,168.  Their shelf faces use 2026/202/2635; crate faces and tops
  use 95/452.  The shelves are *wall geometry with a crate/display surface*,
  rather than low boxes standing in the middle of open floor.
- **S32, cashwrap** — 1,024 × 3,072, floor 6,144 above the surrounding shop.
  The two inset 512 × 512 sectors S33/S34 rise another 2,048 and use floor
  texture 2476 for the cash registers.  Their `floor_stat=122` is part of the
  prefab (flip/repeat-size variant plus relative alignment), as is the shallow
  `floor_heinum=-1792`; omitting either makes the same tile render unlike E6M1.
  Counter base floor is tile 20; its casework uses wood (34), with 35/36 drawer
  details used in the office set.
- **S79, office/back counter** — wood 34, drawer wall 36, plus the chair 758,
  outlet 1050 and clock 1165.  It is a service/work zone, not merchandise
  added to the public aisle.

## Reusable rules

1. Build the shell first: solid shelf banks against a wall, customer route in
   front, and a separate glass display volume.
2. A cashwrap is a three-sector nested prefab: 3,072×1,024 base at +6,144,
   then two 512-square register caps at +8,192.  Preserve the cap's floor
   flags/slope and put the requested wall tile on its portal risers too; a
   top-only 452/2476 patch is not a complete fixture.  Rotate the plan if
   needed; do not squeeze or stretch it.
3. Hang clothes on a solid apparel wall using the source cstat, palette range,
   repeat and height.  Never use them as floor sprites or let them share a
   plane.
4. Put mannequins only in a display sector and preserve their individual
   palette; their three-object rhythm is what sells the window from outside.
5. Treat outlet, clock and chair as service-zone detail.  Their source fields
   are retained in the JSON for the future back-office prefab, not scattered
   into every storefront.

The initial city shop used only the low E6M1 crate module.  The current
`templates.clothier` now takes the measured shelf walls and S32/S33/S34
cashwrap geometry; Theatre Row supplies the clothes and mannequins from their
separate source zones.
